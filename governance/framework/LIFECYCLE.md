# AI Use-Case Lifecycle

## 1. Opportunity Identification

Capture the problem, expected benefit, current process, users, stakeholders, data needs, and why AI may be appropriate.

**Exit:** Intake is valid and complete, the benefit is understood, and AI is an appropriate path for evaluation.

## 2. Opportunity Qualification

Evaluate high-level feasibility, cost and benefit, strategic value, and risk. Route unacceptable or infeasible requests appropriately and communicate the result.

**Exit:** The opportunity has an acceptable preliminary risk profile and a valid business case or explicit strategic rationale.

## 3. Opportunity Prioritization

Compare qualified use cases, confirm capacity and budget, perform technical feasibility reviews, and decide whether to build internally or buy from a vendor.

**Exit:** The use case is selected, technically feasible, appropriately licensed, and assigned a build/buy path.

## 4. Solution Design

Define data, model, human oversight, architecture, security, controls, KPIs, resources, implementation schedule, and vendor requirements where applicable.

**Exit:** Required reviewers approve the design and planned mitigations.

## 5. Solution Development

Prepare approved data, build or configure the solution, train or tune as applicable, integrate it into the intended process, and iteratively validate outputs.

**Exit:** Outputs are valid in context and ready for formal testing.

## 6. Solution Testing

Perform developer testing, trustworthy-AI testing, business-user testing, and a controlled production pilot. Train required users before granting access.

**Exit:** Testing and pilot criteria are satisfied and deployment is recommended.

## 7. Solution Deployment

Complete final training and communications, confirm monitoring and rollback plans, register the solution in the inventory, obtain approval, and release to production.

**Exit:** The solution is deployed with accountable monitoring and support.

## 8. Solution Monitoring & Improvement

Monitor feedback, data access, consumption, cost, performance, model/provider changes, and risk signals. Remediate issues or recommend closeout.

**Exit:** The solution remains in monitored operation or receives a closeout decision.

## 9. Solution Closeout

Resolve contractual commitments, downstream impacts, stakeholder communications, retention obligations, access removal, data disposition, and decommissioning.

**Exit:** The solution is retired or otherwise closed with the outcome and evidence recorded.

## Iteration

Development and testing are iterative. A failed gate returns the use case to the phase necessary to correct the issue; it does not erase the prior decision history.
